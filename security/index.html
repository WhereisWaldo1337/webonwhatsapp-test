Is WhatsApp Web safe?

Yes: WhatsApp Web is end-to-end encrypted like the phone app. The weak points are around it: fake sites, linking scams and computers you leave logged in. Each has a simple fix.

What protects you by design

  • End-to-end encryption on every device. A linked computer gets its own identity key. The sender's app encrypts each message separately for every device you own, so it is readable only on your devices and the recipient's.
  • Linking needs your phone. Nobody can add a computer to your account without your unlocked phone in hand, or without you scanning their code or typing their linking code.
  • Your screen lock guards the link. If the phone has a fingerprint, face or PIN lock, WhatsApp asks for it before it links anything.
  • Warnings for suspicious requests. Since 2026 WhatsApp shows an alert when a linking request looks like a scam, including where the request is coming from, before the device is linked.
  • You see every session. Linked devices lists each linked device and when it was last used, and logs any of them out in one tap.

The real risks, in order

1. Being talked into linking someone else's computer

The most common takeover works without any hacking. A page or message, often from a contact whose account was already taken over, asks you to "vote", "see your photo" or "verify your account". It shows a QR code to scan, or asks for your phone number and then tells you to type an eight-character code into WhatsApp. Both are real WhatsApp linking requests, started from the attacker's browser. Once you complete one, their computer is a linked device on your account: it reads your recent chats and can send messages as you.

Meta warned about this pattern in March 2026, and security researchers at Gen Digital documented the phone-number variant under the name GhostPairing.

Rule: link only on web.whatsapp.com or in an official WhatsApp app, on a device you are holding. Never scan a QR code or enter a linking code because a website, message or caller asked you to.

2. A computer left logged in

Closing the tab does not log out. On a shared, borrowed or office computer, anyone who opens web.whatsapp.com in the same browser profile is in your chats. Log out from Settings, or remotely from your phone.

3. Look-alike websites, unofficial apps and extensions

WhatsApp's advice is to link only to its own apps and web.whatsapp.com, and it says that linking your account to an unofficial app or website can lead to a temporary or permanent ban. Modified apps such as GB WhatsApp fall under the same rule. Browser extensions that promise extra WhatsApp Web features can see everything in the tab.

4. Someone with access to your unlocked phone

Thirty seconds alone with your unlocked phone is enough to link a computer. A screen lock on the phone closes this door, because WhatsApp asks for it before linking.

Settings worth switching on

  1. Screen lock on the phone (fingerprint, face or PIN). It is what protects the link-a-device screen.

  2. Two-step verification on the phone: Settings, Account, Two-step verification. It stops someone registering your number elsewhere. Since August 2026 it is a password of at least eight characters with a letter and a number, no longer a six-digit PIN.

  3. App lock on WhatsApp Web: open Settings, then Privacy, then App lock, and set a password of 6 to 128 characters. Choose automatic locking after 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour or 8 hours, or lock at once with Ctrl + Alt + L (Cmd + Ctrl + L on a Mac). Notifications are hidden while it is locked. App lock exists on WhatsApp Web and in the Windows app, not yet in the Mac app.

  4. Phone notifications for WhatsApp. WhatsApp notifies the phone when a new device is linked; if you did not do it, tap the notification and remove the device.

  5. Review linked devices once a month and after any lost or lent phone.

  6. Code Verify, Meta's open-source extension for Chrome, Firefox and Edge, confirms that the WhatsApp Web code in your browser has not been altered. A green tick means validated. It applies to the browser only, not to the desktop apps.

  7. Security notifications: in WhatsApp Web open Settings, Account, Security notifications and turn on Show security notifications on this computer. You are then told when a contact's security code changes, for example when they add a device. It is set per device.

How to spot a fake WhatsApp Web

  • The address is anything other than web.whatsapp.com. Read it letter by letter; look-alikes swap or add characters.
  • It arrived through an ad, an email or a message rather than you typing the address or using your own bookmark.
  • It asks for your phone number and then for the six-digit registration code that arrives by SMS. WhatsApp Web never asks for that code.
  • It wants a download, a browser extension or a "security update" before showing your chats.

On a work computer

End-to-end encryption protects messages in transit, not what is on the screen. An employer's monitoring software can capture what any window shows, WhatsApp Web included, and downloaded files land on a disk you do not control. For customer conversations a company should run an account it manages: see WhatsApp for business.

What changed, and when

  1. 25 August 2026

    Two-step verification becomes a password

    The six-digit PIN is replaced by a password of at least eight characters with a letter and a number. Passkeys are offered under Settings, Account. Source

  2. 11 March 2026

    Meta warns about device-linking scams and adds an alert

    Scammers trick people into linking the scammer's browser with a QR code or an eight-character code. WhatsApp now shows a warning, with the origin of the request, when a linking attempt looks suspicious. Source

  3. 15 December 2025

    "GhostPairing" is documented

    Researchers at Gen Digital describe an attack that abuses linking with a phone number: a fake page relays WhatsApp's real eight-character code and asks the victim to enter it. Report

  4. 10 March 2022

    Code Verify

    Meta releases an open-source browser extension, with Cloudflare as an independent third party, that checks whether the WhatsApp Web code in your browser is the published one. Source

  5. 14 July 2021

    Multi-device: WhatsApp Web no longer needs the phone online

    WhatsApp describes the architecture that gives every linked device its own encryption keys, so a computer keeps working while the phone is off. It is the basis of today's four linked devices. Source

Questions people ask

Is WhatsApp Web end-to-end encrypted?

Yes. Each linked device connects to WhatsApp independently with its own encryption keys, and messages are encrypted separately for each of your devices, so WhatsApp's servers pass them along without being able to read them.

Can someone read my WhatsApp through WhatsApp Web?

Only if their computer has been linked to your account, which requires your unlocked phone, or you scanning their QR code or typing their linking code. Check Linked devices on your phone: every session is listed there, and you can log out any of them.

Is it safe to use WhatsApp Web on a public computer?

It is a risk. If you must, untick "Stay logged in on this browser" before scanning, use a private window, do not download files, and log out from Settings before you leave. Afterwards confirm on your phone that the device is gone from Linked devices.

Is webonwhatsapp.com safe? Do you see my chats?

This site is a set of guides. It cannot sign you in to WhatsApp, never shows a QR code to scan and never asks for your number or a code, so it never touches your account or chats.

What is Code Verify?

A free, open-source browser extension from Meta for Chrome, Firefox and Edge. It checks that the code running on web.whatsapp.com in your browser matches the version WhatsApp published, with Cloudflare as an independent third party, and warns you if it does not. It does not read your messages.
